# Problem Statement

**Theme:** Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development
**Statement:** Multiple Intermediaries Reduce Farmers' Earnings and Increase Consumer Prices

## Background

India's agricultural marketing system routes most produce through regulated mandis (APMC yards) and unregulated local markets, typically passing through 3–6 intermediary layers before reaching consumers. Farmers often realise only 25–40% of the final consumer price for perishable produce, with the rest absorbed by commission agents, transporters, wholesalers, and retailers.

## Key Challenges

- Multiple margin-taking layers inflate final prices without proportional value addition
- Farmers lack visibility into prevailing market/retail prices
- Distress selling due to inadequate storage and cold-chain infrastructure
- Delayed/uncertain payments from traditional mandi channels
- High transportation costs and 15–30% post-harvest losses from fragmented logistics
- Low digital literacy limiting adoption of existing agri-tech platforms
- No standardised trust/quality-grading mechanism between unfamiliar farmers and buyers
